A mechanical seal is a leak on a schedule.

A conventional pump has a spinning shaft passing through the wall that holds the liquid in. Two polished faces rub together to close the gap. They wear, they run hot when the suction starves, and when they let go the liquid leaves along the shaft.

With water that is a puddle. With hydrochloric acid, toluene or 50% caustic it is a shutdown, a report and somebody in a chemical suit.

Built around the wall, not around the seal.

Three decisions make a Kestrow different from the pump it replaces. None of them needs new pipework.

Nothing rotating crosses the pressure boundary

A ring of magnets on the motor side drags a ring of magnets on the liquid side round with it. Between them sits a one-piece shell, 1.6 mm of alloy C-276 or carbon-fibre-reinforced PFA, bolted up static. No hole for a shaft means no seal, no seal flush and no barrier-fluid pot to top up.

It bolts down where the old pump stood

Every Kestrow follows the ISO 2858 envelope: the same flange positions, shaft height and foot holes as the sealed end-suction pump already on your baseplate. Swapping one is a shift's work for two fitters. You do not cut pipe, and you do not redraw the P&ID.

The whole wet end comes out as one cartridge

Undo eight nuts and the impeller, shell and bearings slide out of the back together while the casing stays in the line. A spare cartridge goes in and the pump is running again in about forty minutes. The worn one comes back to us on an exchange price.

Questions engineers ask before they switch.

Taken from the calls our applications desk logged last year, most frequent first.

What happens if it runs dry?

The bearings are lubricated by the liquid, so a dry pump is a pump on borrowed time. Sintered silicon-carbide bearings with a carbon coating survive about thirty minutes dry. Every pump leaves with a power monitor on the motor that trips it in under five seconds when the load drops, which is long before that.

Is it less efficient than a sealed pump?

Slightly. Eddy currents in a metal shell cost between 4 and 8 per cent of shaft power, and nothing at all with the PFA shell. Against that, there is no seal flush to pump, heat and dispose of. On the duties we have metered, total energy came out within 3 per cent either way.

Can it handle solids?

Up to 5 per cent by weight and 150 microns, with the flushed-bearing option. Above that, or with anything magnetic in the stream, we will tell you not to buy this pump and say what we would use instead.

What if the magnets decouple?

If the impeller jams, the coupling slips rather than breaking something. The same power monitor sees the load fall and stops the motor. The magnets are samarium-cobalt, rated to 250 °C, and are not weakened by slipping once.

Do you sell direct?

Direct in the Nordics, Germany and Benelux. Elsewhere through a service partner who holds cartridges in stock, at the same list price. The duty-point form reaches the same applications desk either way.
